This page covers energy service demand, which means the service people actually want from the energy system, rather than the fuel or electricity used to provide it.

For example, this could mean heat from a heater, hot water from a cylinder, or vehicle kilometres travelled. The gap between energy demand and energy service demand reflects the efficiency of the device providing that service, such as a heat pump, gas boiler, car, or light bulb.

In TIMES-NZ, energy service demand is a key input to the model. The model works out the best way to meet that demand by choosing the mix of technologies that delivers the required service.

Energy service demand is set outside the model. In other words, it is an assumption, not something the model decides for itself. These assumptions are based on drivers such as population, GDP, or land use.

This means energy service demand should usually be read as an input assumption. The model results on this page show which technologies were chosen to meet that assumed level of demand.

Energy service demand is shown in separate charts for different units. Road transport service demand is shown in billion VKT (vehicle kilometres travelled), instead of PJ.
